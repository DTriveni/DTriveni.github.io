## Context

ProblemSolvers Lab was set up around a practical education problem: students often learn concepts in theory but get few chances to apply them to real problems. It began with a six-week pilot involving 30 students and one partner organisation.

I worked with the founder during that early period on programme design, the pilot, school outreach, partnership development and early scaling. I then took responsibility for the organisation’s data, evaluation and monitoring.

## Problem

As the organisation grew to 13 partner organisations and 36 schools, it needed to know whether its programmes were working, and where. Programme objectives were broad, data arrived from partners in different forms, and recurring analysis depended on manual consolidation. There was no existing data team or process to inherit.

## Objective

Build a consistent evidence base for judging programme effectiveness, one that programme managers, partners, donors and leadership can all use, without adding manual work each reporting cycle.

## Approach

The goal was not to collect more data but to make the data that was collected consistent and useful. That meant starting from the programme’s objectives and translating each into measurable indicators, for students, teachers and the programme, before building anything.

## Technical implementation

- **Measurement framework:** indicators for student, teacher and programme outcomes, agreed with programme managers.
- **Reusable trackers and automated workflows** for partner organisations, covering collection, validation, cleaning, standardisation, analysis and dashboard refreshes.
- **Dashboards** for assessment, learning, attendance and programme KPIs. [NEEDS CONFIRMATION: tool]
- **Reporting:** impact, donor and partner reports, beginning- and end-of-year reports, and programme presentations.

## Results

The organisation now has a data and evaluation function that did not exist before: a measurement framework, reusable workflows that reduce manual consolidation, and dashboards and reports used with 24 programme managers.

## Decisions and trade-offs

- **Start from objectives, not data.** Indicators were defined with programme managers first, so dashboards answer questions people actually have.
- **Build reusable workflows over one-off analyses**, so recurring reporting scales with the number of partners.

## Limitations

Programme monitoring data shows what is happening and helps find where to look. On its own it cannot show that the programme caused a change in outcomes.
